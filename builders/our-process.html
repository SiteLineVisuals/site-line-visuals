OUR PROCESS

From the first blueprint to the final walk-through. See how Site Line Visuals bridges the gap between imagination and physical construction.

HOW IT WORKS

Submit your blueprints and complete our guided project intake. We use your plans, selections, and project details to build your 3D home model and prepare it for full-scale visualization.

Timing is confirmed in your proposal based on project size, plan quality, selected services, access, and construction schedule.

THE PERMANENT VISUAL RECORD

A clearer look at how Site Line Visuals supports your home before, during, and after construction.

BEFORE CONSTRUCTION

Blueprints contain critical information, but many homeowners cannot fully understand room size, flow, sight lines, window placement, stairs, ceiling relationships, and major design decisions from flat drawings alone. Site Line Visuals turns the proposed home into an understandable 3D and full-scale VR experience.

Walking through the home before construction begins gives you, your builder, and your design team a clearer way to discuss the plan while adjustments are generally easier to evaluate. The goal is to help you understand what is proposed before major work is installed—not to replace the architect, engineer, builder, or controlling construction documents.

DURING CONSTRUCTION

As construction progresses, important framing, plumbing, electrical, mechanical, insulation, exterior, site, crawlspace, attic, and other accessible conditions can become concealed. Site Line Visuals captures the selected milestones included in your package and approved project scope before those conditions disappear behind finishes.

These visits build an organized visual history of what was accessible and captured at that point in the project. The record helps owners follow progress and creates a clearer reference for conversations with the project team throughout construction.

AFTER CONSTRUCTION

After the home is complete, the captured construction stages remain available as an organized visual reference. Instead of relying only on memory or ordinary finished photographs, owners can revisit documented areas and better understand what was visible before walls, ceilings, insulation, and finishes covered it.

The record can support future maintenance, repairs, remodeling, property understanding, and conversations with qualified contractors. It documents captured conditions; it is not a substitute for destructive investigation, professional inspection, engineering, or code verification when those services are required.

THE FULL JOURNEY

From choosing a path to receiving your finished project record.

1. CHOOSE YOUR PATH & CHECK YOUR ZIP

Choose New Construction or a Custom Request, and confirm your project ZIP is in our current service area before starting the full intake.

2. COMPLETE THE INTAKE

Complete the applicable project details, plan uploads, room choices and reference photos in the mobile-friendly intake. Additional-service requests are reviewed separately against the approved scope. See everything the intake captures →

3. REVIEW & PROPOSAL

We review your submission and issue the controlling proposal or quote — this document sets the exact scope, deliverables, schedule, travel, and add-ons for your project.

4. APPROVE & PAY

Once you accept the proposal, you'll receive a Square invoice or payment request. Scheduling and production are released only after the required deposit/payment is recorded — there's no generic checkout cart.

5. VISUALIZATION & SITE VISITS

We prepare your 3D visualization and coordinate the approved site-capture stages. You experience the proposed home and can request approved decisions or revisions.

6. CONSTRUCTION DOCUMENTATION

During construction, we capture the selected milestones and accessible conditions included in your package, building your Permanent 3D Construction Record.

7. QUALITY REVIEW & DELIVERY

After quality control, you receive your organized deliverables and approved project record.

HOW WE KEEP YOUR COMPANY INFORMED

Agree on the contact, delivery channel and timing before the first visit.

PROJECT KICKOFF

Confirm scope, project contacts, brand kit, client permissions and capture stages. Identify who reviews deliverables and who receives the client handoff.

BEFORE & AFTER EACH VISIT

Coordinate access and stage readiness before capture. Include a visit update recording the capture date, stage, completed scope, access gaps and next action. Set timing in the proposal.

REVIEW & HANDOFF

Provide agreed review materials through the project’s chosen email/file-link channel. Prepare approved client deliverables with your logo, letterhead and a smaller SLV credit.

Levels 1, 2 and 3 include 3, 6 and 12 construction visits respectively. Site verification is outside that allowance. The communication plan includes kickoff, coordination and an update for each completed visit, and final handoff; these are touchpoints, not guaranteed separate calls. Documentation-only and custom visit counts follow the written proposal. Capture records are distinct from formal inspection reports, which require the separately agreed qualified-inspector service.

WHAT YOUR PROJECT INTAKE CAPTURES

Plans, room choices, reference photos and project information—organized before production begins.

Give your clients a structured way to explain the home they want. The intake organizes supplied information by project, room and exterior area so your company and SLV have a clearer starting point for the agreed work. Applicable questions depend on the project and selected service.

1. The project and the people

Client contact details and contact preferences; project address and ZIP; the person’s role; construction stage and schedule; approximate size, floors, foundation configuration and bedroom count; project priorities and areas that need attention.

2. Blueprints and project files

Blueprint uploads, site plans, relevant engineering or structural pages, the current construction schedule and notes explaining the supplied files. Provide the current approved documents; uploading a file does not establish plan approval or replace your controlling construction documents.

3. Windows, doors and trim

Window style and trim preferences by applicable room and exterior area; interior, closet and entry door choices and hardware; door casing, baseboards, crown molding and other applicable trim. These capture supplied design preferences, not an automatically verified window order, dimension schedule or purchasing record.

4. Kitchens, bathrooms and wet bars

Cabinet styles and finishes, countertops, flooring, backsplashes, sinks, faucets, appliance style, range hood and lighting. Bathroom choices include vanities, mirrors, tile or surrounds, showers, tubs, fixtures and hardware. Wet-bar fields include cabinets, hardware, shelving, counters, sink, beverage cooler and lighting.

5. Living spaces and room-by-room design

Flooring, wall paint and texture, accent walls, ceilings, lighting, fireplaces, built-ins and relevant furniture/layout inspiration. Applicable sections cover living areas, bedrooms, office and media room, including media-room window treatments, screen-wall rough-in and control-location preferences.

6. Exterior and outdoor living

Roofing, siding, stone or accent materials, trim, fascia, soffits, windows, entry and garage doors, exterior lighting and foundation finishes. Rear and side areas capture applicable deck/patio surfaces, railing, covered ceilings, outdoor features and landscaping inspiration.

7. Photos, videos and design inspiration

Room-specific inspiration photos or videos help explain the desired appearance. Mobile-friendly uploads let the client provide references where requested. Inspiration guides the agreed visualization; it is not a promise that a pictured product is available or included in the construction contract.

8. Contractors, access and authorizations

General contractor, electrical, plumbing and HVAC company/contact information; supplied insurance documents where requested; site access contact, preferred access times, gates, parking, restrictions and safety concerns. The intake also records the applicable submission and access authorizations.

What your construction company gets

A structured starting point for reviewing the supplied plans, client preferences and references with SLV. Missing details can be clarified before the agreed production work. Your written scope determines what SLV produces and delivers; the intake itself is not an automatic procurement, inspection or change-approval system.

What your client receives

A guided way to communicate their choices and inspiration, followed by the visualization and documentation included in the approved project scope. Company-branded client deliverables are prepared using the agreed brand kit and proof.

How this connects to Home Intelligence

The intake describes the proposed home and supplied preferences. Home Intelligence separately organizes qualifying documented information about installed equipment, appliances, finishes and ownership needs. With the selected service and supporting information, that becomes a professional closeout record for your client. It does not automatically turn every intake preference into a verified installed product.
